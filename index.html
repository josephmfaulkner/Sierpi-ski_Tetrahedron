Sierpiński Tetrahedron

A Sierpiński tetrahedron (or "tetrix") is the 3D counterpart of the Sierpiński triangle. Start with a solid tetrahedron, replace it with 4 half-scale copies of itself — one anchored at each corner — and discard the octahedron-shaped gap left in the middle. Repeat that on each of the 4 copies, forever. The result is a fractal: an infinitely detailed, perfectly self-similar shape with zero volume but infinite surface area, where the same pattern reappears at every scale, no matter how far you zoom.

This project is an interactive, indefinitely zoomable 3D rendering of that fractal, built with three.js. Because the shape is exactly self-similar, the tip at any zoom level is a perfect miniature copy of the whole thing — so rather than pre-building impossibly deep geometry, it renders only a thin "spine" of detail chasing the current zoom target (plus a fixed slice of context to the sides, and, when zooming out, the ancestor shape the view is actually just one corner of), regenerating it on the fly to keep the triangle count small no matter how far in or out you go.
